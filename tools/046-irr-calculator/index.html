What the internal rate of return actually tells you

The internal rate of return (IRR) is the discount rate at which a project's future cash flows are worth exactly as much as the money you put in. Put another way, it is the break-even cost of capital: if your funding costs less than the IRR, the project adds value; if it costs more, the project destroys value. That single percentage is why IRR shows up in almost every investment memo, capital request and venture pitch.

This calculator takes any series of cash flows, solves for the IRR numerically, and then surrounds that number with the figures a decision actually needs — net present value at your own discount rate, a modified IRR that removes the reinvestment assumption, payback and discounted payback, and the profitability index. The NPV profile chart below the results is the most useful of all: it shows how sensitive the project is to the rate you assume.

How the tool works

Enter your cash flows period by period, starting with period 0. The initial outlay is normally negative because it is money leaving your pocket; later periods can be positive or negative as the project earns and then spends again. Choose how many periods make a year so the result can be annualised, set your own discount rate for the NPV comparison, and press Calculate IRR.

Under the hood the calculator searches for the rate where the sum of every cash flow divided by (1 + rate) to the power of its period equals zero. Because that equation has no clean algebraic answer, the tool narrows the range by bisection until the NPV is effectively zero — accurate to many decimal places and, unlike the textbook Newton–Raphson method, reliable even when the cash flow pattern is awkward. If your series never changes sign (all inflows or all outflows), no IRR exists, and the tool says so instead of inventing a number.

Every period's present value and running cumulative total appears in the results table, so you can trace exactly which years carry the project and which drag on it.

Where IRR earns its keep

Capital budgeting. When two machines do the same job but cost and save differently, IRR lets you rank them against the hurdle rate your finance team set. Compare IRR to the cost of capital, not to another project's IRR, unless the projects are the same size and duration.

Real estate. A rental purchase is a negative cash flow at closing followed by rent, expenses and a sale. Solving for IRR turns that messy schedule into one number you can compare with a mortgage rate or a REIT fund.

Equipment and energy projects. Solar arrays, HVAC replacements and fleet upgrades are usually sold on payback, but IRR captures the whole life of the asset, including the residual value at the end.

Fund and deal evaluation. Lenders and investors quote target IRRs constantly. Building the cash flow schedule yourself is the fastest way to test whether a quoted number depends on optimistic exit assumptions.

Pro tips from practice

  • Always read IRR next to NPV. A project can show a handsome 40% IRR on a tiny investment while adding very little absolute value. If the two disagree, NPV is the better arbiter of wealth.
  • Watch for multiple IRRs. When cash flows flip between negative and positive more than once, the mathematics can produce more than one answer. The NPV profile chart exposes this immediately: a curve that crosses zero twice means the IRR is ambiguous.
  • Use MIRR when the reinvestment assumption bothers you. Plain IRR quietly assumes every interim cash flow is reinvested at the IRR itself, which is unrealistic. MIRR reinvests inflows at your discount rate and funds outflows at the finance rate.
  • Keep the time base consistent. Mixing monthly flows with an annual discount rate is the most common error. Use the periods-per-year setting so the annualised figure matches your hurdle rate.
  • Include everything. Working capital, taxes, disposal costs and the salvage value all belong in the schedule. Leaving out the final year is the fastest way to overstate a return.

Common mistakes and FAQ

Is a higher IRR always better? Not automatically. It is better than your cost of capital, and it is a fair ranking tool between similar projects. Between a small high-IRR project and a large modest-IRR one, NPV tells you which adds more money.

Why does the calculator refuse to give me an IRR? The cash flow series must contain at least one negative and one positive value. A series that only ever flows one way has no rate at which it breaks even.

What is a good IRR? There is no universal threshold. Companies typically use a hurdle rate a few points above their weighted average cost of capital to compensate for execution risk, so the answer depends on your funding cost and the project's risk, not on a fixed benchmark.

Why is my IRR so extreme? Very small initial investments paired with long-dated payoffs produce huge percentages that are mathematically correct but practically meaningless. Sanity-check the magnitude of the cash flows, not just the ratio.

Can I compare IRR across different currencies or tax regimes? Only after normalising both. Convert the flows to one currency at a consistent rate and apply the same tax treatment before comparing rates.
