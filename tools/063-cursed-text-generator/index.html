What cursed text actually is

Cursed text is ordinary writing with combining Unicode marks attached to each character. Unicode reserves a large block of "combining diacritical marks" — the same building blocks used to put accents on letters — and those marks are designed to sit on top of, below, or straight through whatever character comes before them. Stack enough of them and a friendly word turns into something that looks like it crawled out of a corrupted file.

There is nothing hidden or dangerous in the output. It is plain text made of standard Unicode code points, so it copies and pastes like any other message. The effect comes purely from volume: a single letter carrying forty stacked marks is tall enough to push the line height of an entire message around it.

How this cursed text generator works

Type a phrase, pick a curse style and set the curse level. The generator walks through your text one character at a time and decides how many marks that character receives. The curse level sets the ceiling; a little randomness decides the exact number so the result looks organic instead of mechanical. The five styles simply bias where the marks land:

  • Cursed — marks above and below in equal measure, the classic heavy look.
  • Haunted — most marks hang below the baseline, like the text is dripping.
  • Static — marks slice through the middle of the letters for a noisy, buzzing feel.
  • Decay — marks sit above only and thin out toward the end of the line.
  • Void — sparse marks with occasional untouched characters, leaving gaps of silence.

Press Re-roll marks to regenerate the same word with a fresh random layout. Nothing leaves your device — the whole generator is a small script running in the page.

Where people use cursed text

The most common use is a reaction. A single cursed word dropped into a group chat lands harder than a paragraph, especially when everyone else is typing normally. Beyond that, creators use it for horror-themed posts, Halloween captions, haunted-house signage, indie game promotions and roleplay handles where a normal font would break the mood. Musicians and streamers use it for track titles and channel art. Writers use it to visualise a character losing their grip on language.

It also has quieter uses. Some people curse a word so a spoiler is unreadable at a glance. Others use a lightly cursed line as a divider between sections of a long post. Used once, it is a punchline. Used everywhere, it becomes noise.

Pro tips for creepy text that still works

Keep it short. Two or three cursed words read as intentional; a cursed paragraph reads as a mistake. Level 3 or 4 usually lands better than level 10, because the reader can still decode the word while noticing something is wrong — that gap between readable and wrong is where the creepy feeling lives. If you want maximum dread, curse the last word of a sentence and leave the rest clean.

Test before you post. Long cursed strings can overflow a caption box, wrap onto extra lines or shove the send button off screen on a phone. If the platform has a character limit, remember that every stacked mark counts toward it even though it adds no visible width.

Common mistakes to avoid

The first mistake is using cursed text for anything that must be searchable or tappable. Hashtags, usernames, links and mentions stop working once the letters are no longer the plain characters the platform expects. The second is assuming it renders the same everywhere. Different operating systems, fonts and apps support different numbers of combining marks; a phrase that looks perfectly cursed on one phone can look almost normal, or collapse into a single dense blob, on another.

The third mistake is forgetting accessibility. Screen readers may try to announce every combining mark, which turns a short phrase into a long, unpleasant stream of noise. If a message carries real information, write it plainly and keep the cursed version as decoration.

Frequently asked questions

Is cursed text safe to copy and paste?

Yes. Cursed text is standard Unicode, not code. Pasting it into a message, a caption or a document cannot run anything or damage your device.

Why does my cursed text look different on another device?

Each device has its own font and its own limit on how many combining marks it will stack before it stops rendering them. A phrase that looks dramatic on one screen may look milder — or heavier — on another.

Will cursed text break my post or caption?

It will not break the platform, but tall stacks can stretch line height and push the layout around. Keep cursed words short and avoid them in fields with strict formatting.

Does this tool save or upload my text?

No. The conversion runs entirely in your browser with JavaScript. Nothing is uploaded, logged or stored, and refreshing the page clears everything.
