What zalgo text actually is

Zalgo text is ordinary text that has been buried under a pile of Unicode combining marks. Each letter in your sentence stays exactly where it is, but small invisible accents get stacked on top of it, pushed through its middle and trailed underneath it. When a font tries to render all of those marks at once, the letters appear to bleed, smear and shiver down the page. The result is the creepy, glitched look that people call zalgo, and it is one of the oldest and best known forms of decorative glitch text on the internet.

The important part is that nothing is broken. Zalgo text is still real text made of standard Unicode characters, so it copies, pastes and searches like any other message. This zalgo text generator lets you control the three directions independently, so you can build anything from a faint haunting to a full meltdown, all inside your browser.

Above, through and below: the three directions

Zalgo is not one single effect; it is three layers that you can mix. Marks placed above a letter look like a crown or a set of antlers, and they raise the top of the line. Marks pushed through the middle cut across the glyph like static or a slash, which reads as broken or corrupted. Marks hung below the letter look like drips, roots or a melting shadow. Classic zalgo uses all three, while "above only" and "below only" give you a cleaner, more deliberate look that survives better in small profile fields.

Because you control each layer separately, you can tune the effect to the space you have. A username usually wants just a few above marks, while a Halloween poster can take a full chaos stack without breaking anything.

Where people use zalgo text

Zalgo shows up everywhere a little unease is useful. Gamers put it in usernames and clan tags so they stand out in a crowded lobby. Social profiles use it in bios and captions to build a creepy or cyberpunk mood. Writers and roleplayers use it to represent a corrupted message, a possessed character or a machine that is failing. Streamers and video editors use it for thumbnails and overlays where a shivering title sets the tone before anyone reads a word.

It is also a favourite for Halloween invites, horror zines, Discord nicknames and sticker or shirt designs, because the look is instantly recognisable and needs no image editing at all.

Pro tips for readable zalgo

  • Keep it short. Two to five words reads as cursed; a paragraph just reads as noise.
  • Lean on one direction. Above-only or below-only stays legible far longer than a full three-way stack.
  • Test the exact field. Paste the result into the bio, username or form you plan to use — some apps trim combining marks.
  • Protect the important word. If people must read a word, curse the edges of the phrase and leave that word plain.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is going straight to maximum marks. A letter carrying dozens of marks can stretch the line height and shove the rest of a layout around, so a profile that looked tidy on one phone can look broken on another. Another trap is assuming every platform accepts the output — several social networks, usernames and form fields quietly reject or strip combining characters. Finally, remember that zalgo text is not reliably screen-reader friendly. If accessibility matters, keep the corrupted version decorative and put the real message in plain text beside it.

Frequently asked questions

Is zalgo the same as glitch text?

Zalgo is the classic, best-known style of glitch text. "Glitch text" is the wider umbrella that also covers static, flicker and fade looks. If you want zalgo specifically, this is the tool.

Will zalgo text show up on every device?

It works anywhere the platform renders combining Unicode marks. Fonts and apps differ, so the exact stacking can shift a little between phones, browsers and operating systems.

Can I use zalgo text in an Instagram bio or a Discord name?

Often yes, but keep the mark counts low. Heavy stacking can be trimmed or blocked by some platforms, so always test with a short sample before you save it.

Does this tool store what I type?

No. Every mark is generated in your browser. Nothing you enter is sent to a server, logged or saved anywhere.
