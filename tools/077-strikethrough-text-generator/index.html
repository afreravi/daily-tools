Why crossed-out text is worth generating

Strikethrough is the oldest editing mark there is, and it survives because it says two things at once: this was here and this no longer applies. In a shared doc that is priceless. A price drop, a corrected date, a task you abandoned — each one reads faster crossed out than explained in a sentence. The problem is that most platforms with a text box refuse to give you the button. Twitter, Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube comments and plenty of chat apps have no strikethrough control at all.

This generator gets around that limitation honestly. It does not take a screenshot or add fake CSS. It inserts a Unicode combining character — technically a stroke overlay that a font draws through the preceding glyph — after every visible character in your line. The result is ordinary text. It copies like text, pastes like text, and keeps its stroke in any app that renders Unicode properly, which today means almost all of them.

How the generator builds each style

Every style in the list is a small recipe. The standard strikethrough uses U+0336, the combining long stroke overlay. The short strikethrough uses U+0335, which many fonts draw as a series of dashes rather than one continuous rule. Swap the stroke for U+0338 and you get a slash through; U+0334 produces a wavy line instead of a straight one. Stacking two strokes gives the heavier double strikethrough, and adding U+0332 underneath produces the strike plus underline look people use for "final answer" jokes.

The bold and italic variants take a different route. They first convert your letters into the Unicode Mathematical Bold or Mathematical Italic blocks, then add the stroke. That means they only work on characters that have a mathematical counterpart — Latin letters mostly, and digits in the bold set. The coverage chart above measures exactly that for your own text, so you can see before you paste whether a style will hold up on a line full of numbers or symbols.

Where people actually use it

  • Social captions and comments. Cross out a claim you are walking back, or set up the classic "I was going to say — never mind" joke.
  • Pricing and promotions. Show a previous price beside the new one in a post where you cannot style text at all.
  • Task lists and changelogs. A struck item reads as finished without an extra word of explanation.
  • Document edits. Mark a superseded figure inside a sentence when tracked changes are not available.
  • Chat and email. Correct a typo visibly instead of silently, which avoids the "wait, what changed?" reply.

Pro tips from regular use

Keep struck lines short. A stroke through a long paragraph becomes visual noise, and on a phone the line breaks fall wherever they like. Strike a phrase, not a page. If you are striking numbers, test the paste in the destination app first — a few apps normalise unusual characters on the way in. Avoid striking inside a hashtag or a mention, because the combining marks can break the link or the search match. And when you need the crossed text to be selectable, searchable and announced correctly by a screen reader, use the HTML <del> snippet instead of Unicode strokes.

Common mistakes to avoid

The first is assuming every device will render the stroke identically. Fonts choose how to draw a combining mark; most draw a clean line, a few draw something thinner or offset. The second is pasting into a field with a strict character whitelist — some username boxes and SMS gateways strip anything outside the basic Latin range, and the stroke vanishes. The third is relying on Unicode text for accessibility. Combining marks are read inconsistently by screen readers, so a strike-through joke can fall flat for a listener. Use it for decoration and visual shorthand, not for information that only the stroke conveys.

Frequently asked questions

Does strikethrough text work on Instagram and TikTok?

Yes, in captions and comments. Both apps render combining stroke overlays, though the exact thickness follows the platform font. Bios are stricter about unusual characters, so paste and check before saving.

Can I undo it once pasted?

Delete and retype. A struck line is a different string from the plain one, so find-and-replace on the original word will not match it. Keep the plain version in a notes app if you plan to reuse the sentence.

Why do some letters look uneven?

Combining marks attach to the character before them. When a font has no glyph for the pair, the browser synthesises one, which can sit a pixel high or low. Switching to the short strikethrough or the slash often looks tidier on a mixed line.

Is the text still searchable?

Only if someone searches for the struck version. Search engines and in-app search index the literal characters, stroke included, so a struck word will not match its plain spelling. That is another reason to keep Unicode strikes for decoration.
